Reading Reflection

After reading “The Rising Risk, and How We Stop It,” I was honestly surprised by how real and close nuclear danger still is today. Before reading this article, I always thought nuclear weapons were mostly a Cold War problem, something from the past. But Hennigan makes it very clear that the risk is growing again, and many countries are getting more nuclear weapons or making more advanced ones. This really changed my understanding of how unstable the world situation is right now.

Something that really stayed in my mind is how many agreements and rules that used to control nuclear weapons are disappearing. I didn’t know countries stopped following some of those safety deals. The article also mentioned that during the Ukraine war, there was a real possibility that Russia might use a nuclear weapon, which honestly shocked me. I always thought nobody would ever actually use one again, because it’s “too crazy.” But now I see the situation is not that simple.

The article also talks about how even one nuclear bomb would cause massive destruction. And I don’t mean just blowing up buildings. It would kill so many people at once, and the radiation would harm people for years and years. Reading the examples and the stories from experts made the danger feel more real, not just theory. I think a lot of young people don’t really think about these things, since we didn’t grow up in the Cold War like our parents. So in a way, we kind of ignore this topic.

This article also made me think about everything happening in the world right now. The conflicts in Ukraine, the Middle East, and the tension between big countries like the U.S. and China all make the situation more dangerous. It’s scary to think that one mistake, or even a misunderstanding, could lead to disaster. Before reading this, I never really thought about nuclear issues in my daily life, but now I feel like young people should pay more attention because it’s our future too.

Overall, this reading opened my eyes. It made me feel that nuclear danger is not just some far-away problem. It’s something real, and it affects everyone. Hennigan’s main message—that hope is not enough—really stuck with me. After finishing the article, I feel more aware of how fragile global safety actually is, and I think more people should learn about this topic instead of just ignoring it.
