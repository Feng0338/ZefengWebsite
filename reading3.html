Reading response for "Motherhood in crisis"

In Motherhood in Crisis, I picked Memunatu’s story “Birth in My Village.” It’s about her giving birth in her village and it was really hard and kind of dangerous. I think the illustrations in this story are really helpful and make the story feel more alive than just photos or text. The colors and lines in the pictures show the tension and loneliness very clearly. The mother’s face and her body movements are drawn in a way that makes you see she’s really tired and scared. Photos are real, but they sometimes have too much stuff in the background and you can’t focus on feelings as much. Text alone tells you what happened, but it doesn’t really make you feel the fear and stress like illustrations do.

Also, illustrations can show feelings in ways that photos can’t. For example, in some parts the background colors are dark to show danger and scary feelings, and bright colors to show hope or safety. This is hard to do with real photos, but illustrations make it easier to understand what the mother is feeling. When text and illustrations are used together, it makes the story easier to get and makes you feel more connected to what’s happening. Photos are real, but sometimes they are too direct or can make people feel uncomfortable, while illustrations don’t do that. They also protect the privacy of the people in the story.

Overall, the illustrations in this story are not just pictures. They help tell the story and show feelings, and they make the reader more involved. Compared to only text or only photos, illustrations give more layers and make it easier to understand the struggles and courage of a mother giving birth in her village. After reading it, I felt like illustrations are really powerful. They let you see what is happening, but also feel the emotions and inner world of the mother. I really liked it and it made me think about how hard it must be for mothers in situations like this.
